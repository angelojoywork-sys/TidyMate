# Tidy mate: product brief

The source of truth for what this product is and who it serves. Design and code decisions are checked against this file. Update it when user testing changes what we believe.

## One line

Track the chores, spare the drama: a shared chore tracker for people who live together, with no account and no download.

## Who it is for

Young professionals sharing a house, sometimes with a student or a couple in the mix. Three to five people, busy on different schedules, friendly with each other but not coordinating closely. First test group: one real house of four, plus four or five friends.

## The problem

Nobody communicates who cleans what. The house needs vacuuming, the downstairs and common washrooms need cleaning, and because daily life is busy nobody says "I did it" or "it's your turn". Jobs get skipped or silently done by the same person, and resentment builds without anyone having argued.

The gap is communication, not motivation. People are willing; they just don't know whose turn it is or whether it already happened.

## What people need to do

1. See at a glance what needs doing and whose turn it is.
2. Log that a chore is done in one tap, and have the house know.
3. Pass the turn to the next person without an awkward conversation.
4. Keep some chores between two people (a couple taking turns on their own washroom or room). These are still visible to the house; they just rotate between fewer people.

## Design principles

1. **The app does the talking.** Every screen should remove a conversation someone would otherwise avoid having. Handoffs and nudges are the core, not an extra.
2. **One tap to log.** If logging takes effort, people stop, and the record stops being trusted.
3. **A house, not a competition.** Show shared progress. No ranking, no shaming, no points against each other.
4. **Calm first, playful second.** Quiet, clear screens; personality shows up in small moments (finishing a chore, the mascot, the copy), never in the way of the task.
5. **Nothing to sign up for.** Joining is a link and a first name.

## Voice

Calm and playful. Plain words, short sentences, a little warmth. Says "Next up: Priya", not "Task reassigned". Never guilt-trips.

## Look and feel

- Light, white-based interface.
- Vibrant, engaging colour. The earlier black-heavy look is rejected; black is for text only.
- Friendly rounded shapes and pill buttons, a mascot, drawn icons for chores.
- Exact palette and type are decided in the design system (see DESIGN.md once it exists).

## In scope now

Chores with an owner, a due date and a repeat; taking turns among everyone or among chosen people; marking done with clear feedback and a handoff; a shared house streak; an activity log; invite by link; up to four people per house.

## Out of scope for now

Live sync between phones (decided later, before real usability testing), push notifications, bills and shopping lists, accounts, a native app, the final mascot artwork.

## Open questions for testing

- Do people log chores without being reminded?
- Is the handoff message welcome or annoying?
- Does a shared streak motivate, or does one late person sour it?
- Do couples actually use the two-person rotation?
